# Silicon Hills Project x EF

## Summit House pilot

UT Austin has not been priced in.

The engineering talent here is world-class. Top labs, AI companies, trading firms, and deep-tech startups already recruit heavily from UT, but the startup infrastructure around the students does not reflect their quality. The best builders are scattered across research labs, technical clubs, West Campus apartments, and coffee shops. Many are already doing work that could become serious companies, but there is no single place built to find them early and bring them together.

That is what we are building with Summit House.

## The house

Summit House is a nine-bedroom house in West Campus that we are converting into 24/7 office and project space for UT's best student builders. Nobody will live there. The bedrooms will become offices, collaborative rooms, and light hardware workspaces.

The house gives students a place to work, but the real value is the density of talent inside it. It sits one stage before a traditional accelerator. We are finding people who are already unusually good at building, including students who may not yet think of themselves as founders, and giving them the peers, accountability, and workspace to take their projects seriously.

## Early demand

The initial response has been much stronger than we expected:

- More than 85 students completed the interest form in the first 48 hours.
- We received 20 inbound partnership and investor conversations, including interest from Rho, Antler, Neo, and other funds, accelerators, and companies.
- We are already partnering with campus organizations such as Texas Venture Group and Genesis, along with the technical clubs and research communities where UT's strongest builders already spend their time.

Students want a serious place to build, and outside organizations want a credible way to reach them.

## The students

The first group already includes people such as:

- **Ali Hussein and Krish Denuka**, both currently at Amazon. Ali previously worked at Jane Street. They are building a financial modeling and quantitative trading platform.
- **Shrey Barmawal**, the youngest-ever employee at Coinbase, who is now working on difficult deep-inference problems.

The broader pipeline includes students building biomedical devices, conducting cancer research, working on brain chips, and developing new physical AI systems. There are many more students at this level across UT. Nobody has built the central community around them.

## The first cohort

We want to begin with a small, heavily curated group of roughly 15 to 20 students. Five to seven hand-selected builders will set the initial culture.

The interest form helps us discover people, but the first cohort will not be built through an open-application free-for-all. We will recruit directly from research labs, engineering teams, technical clubs, and referrals from people we trust. We care about evidence that someone builds because they cannot help themselves: projects shipped, research pursued, hard competitions entered, and technical work done without an obvious résumé payoff.

## Programming

Generic speaker events are easy to run and rarely change how fast a student builds. The core of the program should be recurring accountability with credible people who know what it takes to start a company.

Our working model is:

- Organize students into small pods based on what they are building.
- Match each pod with an experienced founder, operator, or advisor.
- Meet every one or two weeks to review progress, set priorities, and remove blockers.
- Bring in domain experts for targeted working sessions when a team needs specific help.
- End the semester with a showcase for relevant founders, companies, and investors.

We would keep actively investing VCs out of the weekly mentorship loop. Students need room to struggle and improve without every early mistake becoming part of an investment decision. Investors should meet the teams once the teams are ready.

## EF's role

We would like EF to join as a founding programming and sponsorship partner.

The first version could include:

- An EF lead meeting with the cohort or selected pods every two weeks.
- EF helping us design the accountability model and semester programming.
- EF founders, operators, and team members joining targeted sessions when they are in Austin.
- A larger EF founder session or event every couple of months.
- EF participating in the end-of-semester showcase.

Summit House will remain a founder-first community rather than an exclusive funnel for one fund or accelerator. EF's advantage comes from helping build it early and developing real relationships with the strongest students before they enter the usual accelerator and fundraising pipeline.

## Why EF should care

UT has the technical talent to produce far more important companies than it does today. The missing piece is concentration. No organization currently owns the place where the most ambitious student engineers consistently meet, work, and build. Summit House can become that place. If EF wants early access to UT's most ambitious and technically credible students, there is no better hub to help build.

## What sponsorship would fund

We are still scoping the exact first-semester budget with our partners. Sponsorship would fund:

- Desks, chairs, monitors, whiteboards, and meeting-room equipment
- Light hardware and prototyping tools
- Food and supplies that keep students working in the house
- Advisor sessions, founder programming, and community events
- Direct support for promising student projects
- Basic house operations

We would like to define the final sponsorship amount and package with EF based on the programming EF wants to help run.
